Project Background
[Action: Next Slide, speak clearly and naturally]

"PinjamGeng is a responsive web application developed using PHP, MySQL, and Tailwind CSS specifically for student clubs and associations at UPTM. It allows students to check real-time stock availability and submit borrowing requests online 24/7. This eliminates manual paper forms and logbooks, prevents booking conflicts, and ensures organized return handovers."

3
Problem Statements
[Action: Next Slide, highlight points 1 and 2]

"Before building the system, I identified two main problems on campus:
First, the traditional manual paperwork and decentralized process make it difficult to know stock availability, leading to double-bookings, communication misunderstandings, and lost records.
Second, students struggle to identify which equipment items are needed for their specific events, while existing systems only allow borrowing items individually with no pre-bundled event packages."

4
Project Objectives
[Action: Next Slide]

"To solve these issues, I established two core objectives within the 12-week development timeframe:
First, to develop a web-based system enabling students to check stock and submit requisitions online, while administrators manage inventory and approvals.
Second, to provide curated smart event packages for at least four event categories: seminar, workshop, meeting, and photography.
Both objectives were 100% completed successfully within 12 weeks."

Literature Review: Existing Systems
[Action: Next Slide, briefly summarize the 3 reviewed systems]

"Prior to development, I conducted a literature review of three existing asset tracking systems:
1. Cheqroom: Excellent asset bundling and calendar booking, but its enterprise subscription cost is too prohibitive for campus budgets.
2. Booqable: Intuitive booking wizard, but heavily designed for commercial rental businesses requiring invoices, security deposits, and credit card payments.
3. EZOfficeInventory: Full lifecycle tracking with service tickets, but has a steep learning curve and lacks campus-tailored event packages."

Comparison & Research Gap
